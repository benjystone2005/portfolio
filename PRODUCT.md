# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Investment banking and asset management recruiters, hiring managers, and networking contacts (event connections, alumni, referrals) evaluating Benjamin Stone as a candidate. They are fluent in the category: they've seen hundreds of student LinkedIn profiles and generic "aspiring analyst" portfolios. They are scanning, not reading, at first pass, then verifying specifics (numbers, dates, sourcing) if something catches their eye. The job to be done: decide in under a minute whether this candidate is worth a longer look, then find the specific proof points (the Luxury Watch Index, the IAG memo, the Investec pipeline) that justify that decision.

## Product Purpose

A personal recruiting portfolio for a single candidate, Benjamin Stone (second-year Accounting and Finance, University of Edinburgh, incoming IB intern at Investec). It exists to make his self-directed track record (an index he built and maintains, an equity research memo he wrote unprompted) legible and credible to a skeptical, sophisticated audience, and to drive two actions: connect on LinkedIn, or reach out directly. Success looks like a recruiter remembering this candidate specifically, not "a well-designed site."

## Brand Personality

Confident, precise, understated. The site should feel like it was made by someone who already thinks like an analyst, not by someone performing "creative portfolio." Three words: restrained, exact, ambitious. Voice is declarative, not salesy: state the number, let it carry the weight. No hype adjectives, no "elevate/unleash/revolutionize" register.

## Visual References

- **Mercury** (fintech design system) - alpine banking at blue hour, near-black onyx canvas, single cobalt accent reserved for one action, intermediate type weights, pill controls. This is the base design system for the site.
- **Linear** - restraint as a visual argument, whisper-weight type, one accent color doing all the work, motion that is fast and purposeful rather than decorative.
- **Wise** - dark-canvas fintech done with warmth rather than corporate coldness, confident single-accent color discipline.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI-slop SaaS templates (three equal feature cards, centered gradient hero, bento grid of icon tiles).
- Purple / violet gradient accents ("AI glow").
- Centered hero sections as a default.
- Inter used for every text role with no typographic point of view.
- Glassmorphism / frosted-glass panels.
- Stock icon tiles standing in for real content.
- Literal trading-floor iconography (candlestick charts, ticker tape, stock-photo suits and skylines) as decoration - too on-the-nose for this candidate's actual voice, which is quieter than that.

## Design Principles

1. **Restraint is the argument.** The one cobalt accent and the calm, dark canvas are themselves a claim about the candidate's judgment. Do not dilute the accent by using it decoratively.
2. **Every motion has a reason.** Animation should dramatize a specific idea (turning scattered data into one conviction, precision, arrival) not decorate a section because motion is available.
3. **Numbers do the talking.** The proof points (14.36%, 5-10%, EUR 103m, first-class average) are the actual content. Design should make them legible and give them room, not compete with them.
4. **Recruiter-scannable, not recruiter-patronized.** Respect a sophisticated reader: no hand-holding copy, no over-explained metaphors, no filler between the proof points.
5. **One accent, one voice, one system across all four pages.** No section-to-section register drift; Mercury's tokens hold everywhere.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum contrast for all text (ivory/ash on onyx/graphite already verified). Full `prefers-reduced-motion` support: all entrance choreography, scroll-linked 3D, and ambient WebGL/canvas scenes collapse to a static, fully legible state. Graceful degradation on low-power/mobile devices (reduced particle counts, bloom disabled, simpler scenes) rather than frame drops or crashes.
